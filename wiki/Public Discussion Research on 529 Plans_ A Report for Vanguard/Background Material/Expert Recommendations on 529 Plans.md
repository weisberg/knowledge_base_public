# Expert Recommendations on 529 Plans

## Clark Howard (Consumer Advocate & Radio Host)

### Core Recommendations

#### 1. Prioritize Retirement First
**Strong Position:**
> "Wanting to save for your kids' education is great, [but] you shouldn't save a penny for education unless you are already saving the maximum you can for your own retirement."

**Insight:** Parents feel guilt about choosing between retirement and kids' education. Clark addresses this head-on.

#### 2. Age-Based Portfolios Only
**Recommendation:**
> "If you're ready to pick a 529 plan, Clark recommends that you invest in an age-based, direct-sold plan with minimal annual expenses."

**Reasoning:**
- "Set it and forget it" investment choice
- Automatic adjustment from aggressive (stocks) to conservative (bonds) as college approaches
- Similar to target date retirement funds

#### 3. NEVER Use Advisor-Sold Plans
**Strong Warning:**
> "Never buy a 529 plan – never – involving a salesperson. Because the commissions are so bad."

**Key Point:** Direct-sold plans exist, so commission-based plans are "a great way to waste money"

### What This Means for Vanguard

**Positive:**
- Vanguard Nevada 529 is direct-sold ✓
- Offers age-based portfolios ✓
- Low fees ✓

**Challenge:**
- Clark doesn't specifically recommend Vanguard Nevada 529
- His audience trusts his specific recommendations

### Selection Criteria

**Forbes relies on Savingforcollege.com 5-star ratings:**
- Investment performance history
- Fees
- Extra features
- Reliability

### Key Recommendations for Consumers

1. **Review costs** - "529 plans may have administrative fees or other investment costs, which can reduce your investment earnings"
2. **Look into tax benefits** - Compare state tax savings vs. out-of-state features
3. **Understand plan types** - Direct-sold vs. advisor-sold
4. **Review online platforms** - Family contribution features matter

### Plan Highlights

- **New York:** Up to $10,000 annual tax deduction, low fees
- **Massachusetts:** Up to $2,000 annual tax deduction, low fees
- **New Hampshire:** No state tax benefit, but high contribution limit
- **Illinois:** Up to $20,000 annual tax deduction (most generous)
- **Ohio:** Up to $4,000 annual tax deduction for single filers
- **Oregon:** Up to $300 annual tax credit, matching scholarship program

## Common Themes Across All Experts

### 1. Fees Matter Most
- Every expert emphasizes low fees
- Direct-sold plans preferred over advisor-sold
- Expense ratios compared carefully

### 2. Age-Based Portfolios Recommended
- "Set it and forget it" approach
- Automatic rebalancing
- Reduces sequence of return risk

### 3. Retirement Savings First
- Universal recommendation
- Parents feel guilt but need permission to prioritize retirement
- Can't borrow for retirement, can borrow for college

### 4. State Tax Benefits Secondary
- Important but not primary decision factor
- Many experts recommend out-of-state plans if they're better
- Some states (NY, IL) have very generous tax benefits

### 5. Start Early
- Time in market matters more than timing
- Even small contributions grow significantly
- Compound interest is powerful

---

## What Experts DON'T Recommend

### Avoid:
1. **Advisor-sold plans** - High commissions
2. **High-fee plans** - Eat into returns
3. **Overfunding 529s** - Before retirement is secure
4. **Prepaid tuition plans** - Less flexible than savings plans
5. **Complex strategies** - Simple is better for most families
