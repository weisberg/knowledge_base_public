The best 529 savings plans of 2025
Earnings grow tax-free in these state-sponsored investment accounts. Withdrawals are tax-free, too, if the money goes to qualified educational expenses.

Coming up with a strategy to pay for higher education can be overwhelming, but a 529 college savings plan can help parents sleep better at night.

A 529 is a state-sponsored education savings account that allows earnings to grow tax-free. Withdrawals are also tax-free as long as the funds are used for qualified educational expenses, like tuition, room and board, books and computers.

While almost every state has a 529, you’re not limited to your own state’s plan. A family in San Diego can sign up for Utah’s My529 plan, for example, or grandparents in Baltimore can open a New Jersey-based NJBEST plan. (Some states do offer additional incentives for residents, however, including Idaho and New York.)

CNBC Select has reviewed dozens of 529 plans based on fees, expenses, investment choices and more. Here are our top picks to help you maximize your returns for your child’s future.

Formerly known as the Utah Educational Savings Plan, My529 is a direct-sold college savings account available to residents of any state. Account holders can choose risk-based, customizable or age-based portfolios, which automatically rebalance toward more conservative investments as the child nears their college years.

There is no minimum amount to open a My529 and Utah residents are eligible for additional tax benefits.

Illinois’ direct-sold Bright Start College Savings Program is managed by TIAA-CREF Tuition Financing. There are age-based investing options, as well as static and individual options from firms like T. Rowe Price and Vanguard.

There is no minimum amount to open a Bright Star account and Illinois residents are eligible for additional tax benefits.

With the CollegeAdvantage (Ohio) 529 plan, anyone can sign up. There’s a good mix of investment options (including FDIC-insured accounts), low fees and plans from top companies like Vanguard and Fifth Third Bank.

The minimum contribution amount for a CollegeAdvantage plan is only $25 and Ohio residents may qualify for a tax deduction.
